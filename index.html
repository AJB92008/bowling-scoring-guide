🎳 How to Score Bowling By Hand

Ten frames, a handful of symbols, and two rules that trip everyone up at first. Here's how it actually works — plus a scoresheet you can practice on.

01The Basics

Before the scoring rules make sense, get these facts straight.

A game is broken into 10 frames. In each one, you get up to two rolls to knock down all 10 pins. If you knock them all down, the frame ends early — either on the first roll (a strike) or after using both (a spare). What makes bowling scoring unusual is that strikes and spares earn bonus points borrowed from your next rolls — that's the whole trick to learn.

03Scoring Rules

Every frame falls into one of three cases. Frames 1–9 all work the same way:

Open Open Frame

Neither a strike nor a spare — pins are left standing after two rolls. The frame score is just the two rolls added together. No bonus, no borrowing from the future.

frame score = roll 1 + roll 2

Example: you roll a 7, then a 2. Frame score is 9. Simple.

2

/ Spare

You knock down all 10 pins using both rolls. The frame score is 10, plus whatever you roll on your very next ball (the first roll of the next frame) as a bonus.

frame score = 10 + next 1 roll

Example: you roll 6 then 4 (a spare), and your next roll is a 5. That frame scores 10 + 5 = 15. Notice the frame's score isn't final until you see that next roll — that's why scoresheets often show a spare frame as "pending" until the following ball is thrown.

/

X Strike

You knock down all 10 pins on the first roll, so there's no second roll in this frame. The frame score is 10, plus your next two rolls — wherever they happen to fall (both could be in the next frame, or split across the next two frames if that next frame is also a strike).

frame score = 10 + next 2 rolls

Example: you strike, then in the next frame roll 4 and 3. That frame scores 10 + 4 + 3 = 17. If instead you strike again next frame, you have to wait one more frame to find out the second bonus roll — this "waiting" is exactly why a string of strikes (like in a perfect game) only gets totaled up at the very end.

04The 10th Frame — the Exception

The last frame breaks the "borrow from the next frame" rule, because there is no next frame to borrow from. Instead, bonus rolls happen inside the 10th frame itself.

If you open the frame (no strike or spare): you get your normal two rolls, and the pins don't reset. Score is just the two rolls added together, same as any open frame.

If you spare (10 pins across two rolls): you get one extra bonus roll, with the pins reset to a fresh 10. Score is all three rolls added together.

This is also the secret behind a perfect game (300): twelve strikes in a row — one in each of the first nine frames (each worth 10 + the next two rolls, which are always also strikes = 30), plus three in the 10th frame (10+10+10 = 30). Twelve strikes, ten frames, 300 points.
